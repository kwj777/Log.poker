I looked down at two kings and went all in before the flop. Everybody folded but one player, who called.

He turned over a five and a six of the same suit.

The flop had a nine on it. The turn was a seven. The river was an eight. Five, six, seven, eight, nine: a straight, and the pot went the other way.

::: figure kings-into-a-runner-runner-straight
I have the ranks but not the suits, so those are dashed. I do not remember the other two cards on the flop, only that the nine was there.
:::

If you play, you already know how this one feels. If you do not, this is the hand every player has a version of: the second best starting hand in the game, all the money in the middle, and a hand that should have been in the muck catching exactly the two cards it needed.

## Where it stood before the flop

Once both hands are face up, there are 48 cards left and the dealer is going to put five of them on the board. That is 1,712,304 possible boards, and the question is how many of them each of us wins.

I ran it. Kings against five six suited win about 77 percent of the time, a little more if one of my kings shares a suit with his cards, because that takes a flush card away from him. He wins about 22 percent, and the rest is a split.

::: stat 77 percent
How often two kings beat five six suited when they are all in before the flop.
:::

So I was better than a three to one favorite with everything in. That is the spot you sit at a poker table hoping for. You do not get to choose your cards, but when you get kings and somebody puts their stack in against them with five six, the money went in exactly where you want it.

## The way he got there

That 22 percent is spread across a lot of ways to win: a flush, two pair, trips, and a handful of straights. He got one of the narrow ones.

After the flop, with a nine on the board and two cards that I will assume did not help him, he needed exactly a seven and an eight on the turn and river, in either order. There are 45 cards he has not seen, and 990 ways to pick the last two. Four sevens and four eights make 16 of those pairs.

16 out of 990 is about 1.6 percent, or roughly 1 in 62.

That is what runner runner means. Not one good card, two of them in a row, both from a short list. It happens. It happened. It is still a 1 in 62 shot from the flop, against a hand that was already a big underdog before the flop came down.

## What I am taking from it

There is no lesson about how I played it, and I am not going to invent one. Kings all in before the flop against one caller is the result of a good decision, not the decision itself. The decision was made, and it was right, the moment the chips went in at 77 percent.

What the river decided was the pot, not whether I played well. Those two things feel like the same thing at the table. They are not, and the gap between them is where tilt lives. If I let the eight on the river tell me something about my kings, the next time I get them I play them worse.

The honest math is this: get it in with kings against five six a hundred times and you lose it about twenty two times. This was one of the twenty two. Over enough of them, the other seventy seven pay for it, and nobody remembers those.
