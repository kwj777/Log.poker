Observe

Running bad, or playing bad?

It is the question every losing stretch provokes, and it has an answer. Not a certain one, but a useful one: whether a stretch like this is ordinary for a player with your win rate and your swings. For most rough stretches, it is.

The short version

Poker results are a random walk with a small drift. Over a stretch of h hours, a player who wins w big blinds an hour with a standard deviation of s big blinds an hour finishes with an expected w × h and a spread of about s × √h.

The drift grows with time. The spread grows with the square root of time. That single mismatch is the whole story of a downswing: over a weekend the spread dwarfs the drift, and over a year the drift wins.

What ordinary looks like

Take a live winner at 5 big blinds an hour with a standard deviation of 50. Those two numbers are an example and nothing more, but the shape they produce is the shape everyone's looks like.

After Expected 95% band Chance of being behind
20 hours +100 bb -338 to +538 bb 32.7% (1 in 3)
50 hours +250 bb -443 to +943 bb 24.0% (1 in 4)
100 hours +500 bb -480 to +1,480 bb 15.9% (1 in 6)
250 hours +1,250 bb -300 to +2,800 bb 5.7% (1 in 18)
500 hours +2,500 bb +309 to +4,691 bb 1.3% (1 in 79)
1,000 hours +5,000 bb +1,901 to +8,099 bb 0.1% (1 in 1,278)

The 95% band is where 19 stretches out of 20 finish. Anything inside it is unremarkable, which is a different claim from "good".

Read the last column again. This player is a genuine winner, and after a hundred hours at the table they are still behind one time in six. Not because anything went wrong. That is simply what 100 hours of this much variance looks like.

A hundred hours is two or three months of playing a couple of nights a week. Most players have never had a sample long enough to be confident about their own win rate, and have had many samples long enough to feel certain they are broken.

Why the answer has to use your numbers

The table above is somebody else's. Two players at the same stake can have standard deviations 40% apart, and the band scales directly with it: the loose aggressive player's ordinary month would be a crisis for the nit and the nit's would be a quiet week for them.

There is no way around measuring your own. You need the result and the duration of each session, in big blinds so that stakes pool together, and you need enough of them that the estimate is not itself noise. Twenty hours and eight sessions is the floor where the numbers start to mean something, and it is a floor rather than a target.

One thing the arithmetic cannot do: prove you are playing well. A stretch inside the band is consistent with running bad and with a leak that costs you less than the noise hides. What it rules out is the panic, which is worth a great deal on its own, because the panic is what makes the leak.

What to do with a stretch that is outside the band

One stretch in forty finishes below a 95% band, so seeing one is not evidence of much. Two things are worth doing anyway, and neither is quitting.

  • Check the sample, not the story. Was it one stake or four? One game type? A run of sessions that all started after midnight? A band computed across conditions you no longer play in is answering a question you did not ask.
  • Protect the bankroll before the ego. A stretch this cold does not predict the next one, but it has already changed how much cushion you have. That is an arithmetic problem with an arithmetic answer.

And the thing that is genuinely not variance: the decisions you make in the ten minutes after a hand goes wrong. Those are not in any band, and they are the only part of a downswing you control.
