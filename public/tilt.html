Tilt is a state before it is a decision

Everything on the other pages here is arithmetic about things you do not control. This is the one page about the part you do.

The arithmetic of it

A bad beat costs you the pot, and the pot is the small number. Say it takes two hours to come back to yourself, and over those two hours you play three big blinds an hour worse than usual: wider preflop, one more thin call, one bluff at somebody who was never folding.

That is six big blinds, on top of the pot, off a hand that was already decided. Do it twice a month and you have given back the win rate the bankroll math was counting on, and the band will not show it to you as a leak, because it looks exactly like variance from the outside.

This is the asymmetry worth sitting with. Variance is large, unavoidable and eventually neutral. Tilt is smaller, avoidable and never neutral. Almost all the attention goes to the first one.

What is actually happening

Tilt gets discussed as a character failing, which is both unkind and useless. What it is closer to is a physiological state: a stress response, with a heart rate and a breathing pattern and a narrowed attention, that arrived before you had an opinion about it.

That matters for one practical reason. You cannot reason your way out of a state you did not reason your way into. Telling yourself the call was correct does not work, because the part of you that needs convincing is not listening to arguments. What does work is changing the state directly, and the state has physical inputs.

What works at the table

  • Sit out one orbit. Not as punishment. The single most reliable predictor of a tilt hand is that it came within a few minutes of the last one, and standing up breaks that.
  • Breathe out longer than you breathe in. The crudest possible intervention, and the one that acts on the state rather than on the story about it. A long exhale is a direct lever on the system that is currently running you.
  • Name the number. "That was a 4 big blind pot" is not a coping mechanism, it is a correction. Tilt inflates the size of what just happened, and saying the real figure out loud deflates it faster than reassurance does.
  • Decide the stop-loss before the session. A limit set in advance is a decision by somebody who was not upset. A limit set at the time is a negotiation you will lose.

And the one that is not a technique: notice it earlier. Almost nobody catches tilt at the start, because at the start it does not feel like tilt. It feels like clarity, and a sudden certainty about what somebody else has.

What does not work

  • Playing through it to prove a point. The point gets proved to nobody and costs the six big blinds above.
  • Reviewing the hand immediately. Do review it. Not now: the review you do while upset finds the answer you are already committed to.
  • Moving up to get it back. Larger stakes have larger swings, and the risk of ruin formula squares that term. This is the single fastest way to turn a bad night into a bad year.
